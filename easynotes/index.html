Get started

Introduction

EasyNotes is a Windows desktop app for the notes you keep while you work: meeting actions, specs, interview notes, checklists, trackers and research, organised into topics. A topic holds sub-topics and pages; a page is made of blocks – headings, lists, tables, checklists, callouts, pictures, shapes, code – and is saved the moment you type. There is no Save button.

It lives in the system tray, comes to the front with Ctrl + Shift + Space and keeps the small things within reach: the twenty pages you touched last, what you copied ten minutes ago, the to-do buried in yesterday’s notes, the pieces you need to carry between apps. It imports the documents you already have – Word, PDF, Excel, PowerPoint, web pages, OneNote – exports to PDF, Word, Markdown and HTML, backs everything up on a schedule, and gives your AI tools a local, rule-bound way in.

For IT administrators

EasyNotes ships Group Policy (ADMX/ADML) templates that work with Intune and other MDMs. They can set a default, block or enforce: MCP and its rules, port and policy file, the session clipboard, scheduled backups, the backup folder, frequency and retention, and the notes folder. Managed controls show “Managed by your organisation” and cannot be changed by the user. An enforced MCP setting that cannot be read from a trusted file keeps MCP off.

First launch

The first time EasyNotes starts, a short splash leads into a seven-step setup. Every choice can be changed later from the gear in the header, and Cancel never leaves you stranded: if you stop early, your notes go to a safe local folder that you can change in Settings › Storage.

  1. Welcome – what EasyNotes does, in three cards.
  2. Storage – “Do you already use EasyNotes on another PC?” Pick up an existing synced folder, or start fresh in a folder you choose. See User Data.
  3. Import – optionally open the OneNote import straight after setup.
  4. Backup – automatic backups, the backup folder, frequency and retention.
  5. Session Clipboard – off unless you switch it on.
  6. Appearance – Follow Windows, Light or Dark, and optionally your Windows accent colour.
  7. Windows – Start at sign-in (minimised to the tray), the Start menu entry and the global hotkey Ctrl+Shift+Space. All in your own user context – no admin rights, no stored password.

User Data path and recommendation

EasyNotes keeps two folders apart: one for your notes, one for the app’s own preferences and logs.

User Data (your notes)index.json (topics and page list), one file per page – pages\<id>.page.json for a page, .csv for a spreadsheet – its pictures, videos, PDFs and attached files in pages\assets, Transit in scribble, deleted items in trash, and a copy of your reminders
App Data (the app)Preferences, the app and backup logs, the MCP audit log, notifications and backup history. %LOCALAPPDATA%\EasyNotes, or the package’s own storage when EasyNotes is installed as a package
TemporaryThe session clipboard and undo history, under App Data – deleted when EasyNotes exits
Recommended: a folder that OneDrive, Google Drive or Dropbox syncs

A synced folder keeps a copy off the PC, so a drive failure doesn’t take your notes with it, and makes them available on your other PCs. EasyNotes has no cloud of its own – it uses the sync app you already have.

During setup EasyNotes looks for the syncing locations on the PC and offers them as tiles:

  • OneDrive – personal and work or school accounts.
  • Google Drive – folders on this PC that Google Drive syncs. The streaming drive (G:) is left out: it holds cloud content, not folders on this PC.
  • Dropbox – personal and business.
  • Other folder – any local, offline or undetected location. EasyNotes warns when a folder is on this PC only.

With one syncing location, <that folder>\EasyNotes is pre-filled. With several, nothing is pre-selected so you choose deliberately. With none, the default is %USERPROFILE%\EasyNotes. For OneDrive and Dropbox, setup also sets Always keep on this device on the folder, so your notes open instantly and work offline.

Using EasyNotes on two PCs

Point both PCs at the same synced folder. On the second PC, the Storage step finds it (“Your EasyNotes data is already here”) and carries on with every topic and page. Changes from both PCs are merged, and sync-conflict copies are merged automatically.

Changing the folder later

Settings › Storage › Change… offers a backup first (Backup & Proceed), then EasyNotes opens the new folder. It does not move or copy notes, and nothing is deleted from the old folder – it is left exactly as it is.

Backup: path, frequency and retention

Backups are on by default. Your notes and preferences are zipped on the schedule you choose, verified, and old archives are tidied away.

FolderOneDrive\Documents\EasyNotes_Backup when OneDrive is signed in, otherwise Documents\EasyNotes_Backup. Any folder outside your notes folder can be chosen
FrequencyDaily, every 1–7 days (default: every day), or Hourly, every 2, 4 or 8 hours
Retention1–90 days (default 30). Older archives are deleted at the next launch
File nameEasyNotes_Backup_MM-DD-YYYY-HH-MM.zip – an existing archive is never overwritten
What’s insideThe whole notes folder (including media, trash, Transit and reminders), your preferences, the app log and a manifest. Never the session clipboard

Each archive is written as a partial file, re-opened, and checked entry by entry before it gets its final name. Backups run at launch when one is overdue and on schedule after that. If one fails, EasyNotes retries every 30 minutes and lights the bell in the header until a backup succeeds.

The folder hint tells you what the location protects against – for example “Synced to OneDrive - copies also leave this PC”, or “On this PC only, same drive as your notes – protects against mistakes, not against losing this PC or its drive”.

Back up now and restore

Settings › Backup has Back up now, the last 30 runs in Backup history and Open backup log. Restore from backup… checks the archive first, renames your current notes folder to …_before-restore_<date> – nothing is deleted – and reloads the restored notes. Preferences are not changed by a restore.

Import documents and OneNote

Bring in what you already have. The green Import tile above the topic tree (Ctrl+Shift+I) opens a menu: Any document, PDF, Word, Excel workbook / CSV, PowerPoint, web page, code file, or OneNote notebooks. The same Import a document is the fourth tile of the New page dialog (Ctrl+N) and sits on every topic’s ＋ and right-click menu, where the topic is already chosen.

  1. Pick the files – several at once is fine, of mixed types.
  2. Pick the topic – every parent, sub-topic and child topic is listed; type to filter, or make a New topic… on the spot.
  3. Watch it land – a progress card shows each file; the first page opens when the import is done. Problems (a file that could not be read, an old .doc / .xls / .ppt) are shown in a dialog; routine notes, such as a hidden worksheet skipped, go to the bell.
Word (.docx)Headings (Title, Heading 1–3), bold / italic / underline / strike / highlight / colour / links, bullet and numbered lists with their levels, tick boxes, tables (header row kept), pictures, code in a monospace font, quotes; page breaks become sections. Text boxes, comments and footnotes are not brought across
PDFThe PDF stays viewable at the top of the page. Below it every PDF page is its own section: a “Page N” heading, the text as paragraphs (large text as headings, bullet and numbered lines as lists) and the embedded pictures. Scanned pages have no text to lift out; drawn charts stay in the PDF block
Excel (.xlsx), CSV, TSVEvery visible worksheet with content becomes a Spreadsheet page named “workbook - worksheet” (one worksheet: the workbook’s name). Cells arrive as Excel shows them – dates, booleans, numbers, the results of formulas. Hidden and empty worksheets are skipped and noted. A CSV or TSV file becomes one Spreadsheet
PowerPoint (.pptx)One page per deck. Every slide is its own section – “Slide N - title”, the slide’s text and bullets by level, tables, pictures, and the speaker notes as a note callout. Hidden slides are skipped. Charts and SmartArt are not brought across (noted)
Web page (.html, .htm)A saved page, including Word’s “Web page” format: headings, formatting, links, lists, tick boxes, tables, code, quotes, and pictures saved with the page or embedded in it. Pictures still on the web are left as links – nothing is fetched
Code (.ps1, .sh, .py, .json, .xml, .ini, .yaml, .sql, .cs …)Each file becomes a Code page in the language its extension says (some 60 extensions; a file with no extension such as a Dockerfile is recognised too). The text arrives exactly as it is, BOM-aware. A .html is a web page unless Code file… is the entry you chose
LimitsFiles up to 200 MB (code files 20 MB); worksheets up to 20,000 rows × 256 columns; PDFs up to 300 pages of text

The original files are never changed. An AI tool can run the same import with the import_document MCP tool.

OneNote notebooks

Choose OneNote notebooks… from the Import menu (Ctrl+Shift+O), or tick it during setup. It needs the OneNote desktop app (OneNote 2016 or Microsoft 365): a notebook becomes a topic, a section a sub-topic (a section in a section group is named “Group › Section”), a page a page.

Comes across as it is

  • Text with its font, size, colour, highlight and emphasis
  • Bullets, numbering and indent levels
  • Tables with column widths and cell shading
  • Links, including links back into OneNote
  • Pictures as picture blocks; to-do tags as real tick boxes

Changes on the way

  • Handwriting and drawings become a picture of the page
  • Inserted files become file, PDF or video blocks with the file in the page’s media
  • Other tags become symbols (★ ⚑)
  • Audio, page history, author stamps and password-protected sections are not imported

You see all of this before anything is read. Then you choose what to bring in: everything is ticked; untick what you would rather leave behind, or clear Select all and tick just the sections – or single pages, behind each section’s arrow. OneNote is only read, never changed. Anything that could not be converted is listed on an Import report page in each imported topic, so nothing goes missing quietly.

MCP setup

The Model Context Protocol lets AI tools – Claude Desktop, Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, GitHub Copilot in VS Code, Cursor and any other MCP client – read and write your notes through the running app. It is off by default, reachable from this PC only, and every client needs its own token. Every call is logged, and every write can be shown to you.

One setup, any AI tool. The steps below use Claude Desktop as the worked example. The same settings work in every MCP client: each one needs the same three values – only the file they go in differs (see the table under the steps).
  1. Enable it. Open Settings › MCP (or click the bot in the header) and switch on Enable MCP integration. EasyNotes starts a local endpoint on http://127.0.0.1:47391/mcp while it runs; the port can be changed.
  2. Create a token for each client. Name it, pick a scope and click Create token. The token is shown once – EasyNotes keeps only its hash. Give each tool the smallest scope it needs: read is enough for questions and summaries.
  3. Copy the config. Under Connect an AI tool, click Copy. The block holds the standard config with EasyNotes’ exact path and the new token – masked on screen, copied in full. Most AI tools read this format; the few that use another are in the table below. A copy that holds a token is kept out of Windows clipboard history and the cloud clipboard.
  4. Paste it and restart the tool. For Claude Desktop: Settings › Developer › Edit Config opens claude_desktop_config.json; paste the block (if the file already has "mcpServers", add only the "easynotes" entry inside it), save, quit Claude Desktop from the tray icon and start it again.
  5. Check it. Ask the tool to read your EasyNotes rules. It calls get_rules, and the call appears in the audit log.
  6. Write your rules (optional). Plain-language instructions that every AI tool receives the moment it connects – see Rules for AI tools.

The same three values, in every tool

commandThe full path of EasyNotes.exe – the config in Settings › MCP has the exact path of your install
args--mcp – EasyNotes runs as a small stdio relay to the running app, and starts it minimised if it isn’t running
envEASYNOTES_MCP_TOKEN = your token – it travels in an environment variable of that process, never on its command line
AI toolWhere the config goesFormat
Claude Desktop%APPDATA%\Claude\claude_desktop_config.json (Settings › Developer › Edit Config)JSON, "mcpServers"
Claude CodeOne command in a terminal, for every projectclaude mcp add
OpenAI Codex (CLI and IDE extension)%USERPROFILE%\.codex\config.tomlTOML, [mcp_servers.easynotes]
GitHub Copilot in VS CodeYour user mcp.json (MCP: Open User Configuration)JSON, "servers" – the token is asked for once and kept in VS Code’s secret storage
Cursor%USERPROFILE%\.cursor\mcp.jsonJSON, "mcpServers"
Any other MCP clientWhere its documentation says (often mcp.json)Usually JSON, "mcpServers"

Claude Desktop, Cursor and most clients – JSON

{
  "mcpServers": {
    "easynotes": {
      "command": "<path to>\\EasyNotes.exe",
      "args": ["--mcp"],
      "env": { "EASYNOTES_MCP_TOKEN": "<your token>" }
    }
  }
}

GitHub Copilot in VS Code – the token stays out of the file

{
  "inputs": [
    {
      "type": "promptString",
      "id": "easynotes-token",
      "description": "EasyNotes MCP token",
      "password": true
    }
  ],

    "easynotes": {
      "type": "stdio",

OpenAI Codex – TOML

[mcp_servers.easynotes]
command = '<path to>\EasyNotes.exe'
args = ['--mcp']
env = { EASYNOTES_MCP_TOKEN = '<your token>' }

Claude Code – one command

claude mcp add --env EASYNOTES_MCP_TOKEN=<your token> --transport stdio --scope user easynotes -- "<path to>\EasyNotes.exe" --mcp

Keep tokens safe. Claude Desktop, Claude Code, Codex and Cursor keep the token as plain text in a file in your user profile – VS Code keeps it in its own secret storage. Never commit a config that holds a token to a repository, give each tool its own token with the smallest scope, and revoke a token in Settings › MCP the moment a tool no longer needs it. If a tool reports that EasyNotes rejected the access token, create a new token and copy the config again from Settings › MCP. Changing the port needs no change in any tool: the relay reads it from EasyNotes.

Token scopes

readLook only – topics, pages, search, backlinks, page history, reminders, Transit, the session clipboard, backups and settings
write (default)Also create, change, append, move, rename and open pages and topics, import documents, set cells, add reminders, insert pictures and shapes, use Transit and the clipboard, run a backup
fullAlso delete and clear. Deleted pages still go to the notes folder’s trash

Rules for AI tools

Rules reach the AI when it connects, through the get_rules tool, at the top of every write tool’s description and again after every write. Here is an example – the rules used in the workspace shown on this page:

Example

1. Never read, change or delete anything under the topic “Personal”.

2. Never change my existing pages. Put your version on a new page right below the page you worked from, with the title prefixed “Claude - ”.

3. Keep my headings and bullet style; British English.

Write any rules your work needs, in your own words, in Settings › MCP – what to leave alone, where new work goes, the style to keep. Insert example there gives you a starting point.

Rules are requests to the AI, not locks: a tool can still ignore them. Token scopes are the hard limit, and every call – read or write – stays in the audit log.

The page editor

A page is a flowing document made of blocks. Click anywhere and type; click in the empty space below the text and the caret goes there, indented to where you clicked. The toolbar along the top has the formatting group on the left and the coloured tools on the right.

Page zoom

Every page, spreadsheet and checklist zooms from 50% to 200% in 10% steps: the slider in the middle of the row under the header (with − and +; click the percentage to go back to 100%), or Ctrl + mouse wheel over the page. The zoom is remembered; the topic tree keeps its own text size.

Blocks and the “/” menu

Type / on an empty line (or use Insert) for every block: Text, Heading 1–3, Bulleted list, Numbered list, To-do list, Checklist, Toggle, Quote, Callout, Date & time, Icon, Link to a page, Table of contents, Divider, Image, Pictures side by side, Video, Audio, PDF, File, Table, Code block. Markdown shortcuts work as you type: # for a heading, - for a list, 1. for numbering, > for a quote.

Formatting

Select text for bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, font and size, highlight and text colour; the format painter copies all of it onto the next thing you select. Bullets come in nine list styles and twenty symbol bullets – real lists, so they nest with Tab / Shift+Tab. Symbols and colour emoji live behind the Ω button, with search and a recently-used row. Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y keep working after you leave a page and come back – the history lasts the whole session.

Sections and the block handle

Point at a line and a handle appears to its left: ＋ adds a line below and opens the / menu; the dotted grip works on the section the line is in – the run of blocks with no gap, or single blank lines, between them (two blank lines separate sections). Hover it to see the section framed; click it to select the whole section; drag it to move the section, with a blue line showing where it will land. Ctrl while dropping copies; Shift moves just that line. An imported deck arrives as one section per slide, so a whole slide moves in one drag.

Pictures, side by side

Paste a picture, drop one from Explorer, or use Insert › Image. Click a picture to select it and drag a corner to resize. Right-click a picture › Put side by side with the picture next to it makes a row of two to four pictures; drag the bar between them to change the split; Stack the pictures undoes it. Text and pictures select together, so copying a paragraph takes its pictures with it.

Shapes

Draw a shape opens a catalogue of 179 shapes in seven groups – Basic, Arrows, Flowchart (the standard set), Callouts, IT (servers, routers, firewalls, users, locks, clouds…), Diagram (actor, class, state, swimlane…) and Marks – with a search box. Pick one, then drag on the page where it should go; a click gives its natural size. Click the outline to select it: drag to move, corner grips to resize (Shift keeps the proportions), arrow keys nudge. The shape bar sets outline colour, fill, line weight and text size; double-click (or just type) to write inside it.

A shape belongs to the paragraph it was drawn on – it moves, copies, prints and undoes with it, and the paragraph makes room so words never hide under it. Drop a shape on the upper half of a block and it anchors there with the words below it; drop it on the lower half and it gets a paragraph of its own after that block.

Icons

Insert an icon, /icon or right-click › Insert icon… opens about 1,850 line icons, searchable by everyday words (warning, done, user, cloud, shield, deadline…). An icon sits in the line like a character – it takes the text colour and size, plus its own scale (1×–4×). Select one and open the picker again to swap, recolour or resize it.

Page links, backlinks and contents

Type @ and part of a page’s name, then Enter: a page chip that opens the page with a click and follows renames and moves. Under every page, Linked from N pages lists the pages that link to it. Table of contents (/toc) keeps a live list of the page’s headings. Web links, \\server\share and C:\ paths, and “Topic › Page” paths become links too (Ctrl+click); folders open in Explorer, documents with their app – anything that could run is only shown in Explorer, never started.

Find and replace, date and time

Ctrl+F opens a find bar in the page (count, next / previous, match case, whole word); Ctrl+H adds Replace, Replace all (one undo) and Replace selected. Ctrl+Shift+D (or /date) stamps the date and time.

Print and export

The printer button (Ctrl+P) prints the page with a header (title, topic, date), or exports it: PDF, Word (.rtf), a one-file web page (pictures embedded), Markdown (with a media folder for the pictures) or plain text. Icons and shapes are printed; toolbars, menus and reminder bells are not. Printing always uses the light colours, whatever theme is on screen.

Spreadsheets

A Spreadsheet page is a plain-value grid: type-ahead filters on every column, sort by clicking a caption, rename columns in place, insert and delete rows and columns, fill down (Ctrl+D), paste one value onto a selection, move rows with Alt+↑ / ↓, copy a cell, a row, a column or the whole sheet, and export to XLSX or CSV. Imported workbooks arrive one worksheet per page.

Code pages

A Code page is Monaco (the open-source code editor), bundled offline inside EasyNotes and run in the same locked-down sandbox as the page editor: no network, nothing on disk to tamper with, and nothing in a code page is ever executed – a .ps1 is text and stays text. Pick Code on the New page dialog (or Code… on a topic’s ＋ menu), choose a language or leave it on Detect automatically: paste a script and the colours follow.

  • 39 languages with syntax colours – PowerShell, Bash, Batch, Python, JSON, XML, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, TypeScript, INI, YAML, Markdown, SQL, C#, C / C++, Java, Kotlin, Swift, Go, Rust, Ruby, PHP, Perl, Lua, R, Dart, Scala, F#, VB, Dockerfile, GraphQL, Bicep, Terraform, Azure CLI, Protobuf and plain text. Detected from what you type or paste; change it any time from the toolbar or the status strip.
  • A real code editor’s editing – find and replace (Ctrl+H), go to line, multi-cursor (Alt+click, Ctrl+Shift+L), bracket pair colours and guides, folding, sticky scroll, word highlight, word-based suggestions, JSON validation, the command palette (F1).
  • The toolbar – undo / redo, find, replace, go to line, outdent / indent, line comment, Format (JSON, HTML and CSS fully; XML too; other languages re-indented and trimmed), a Lines menu (move, duplicate, delete, join, sort A–Z / Z–A, remove duplicates, UPPER / lower, trim trailing whitespace, select all occurrences, fold / unfold all), toggles for word wrap, minimap, line numbers and whitespace, and the coloured group every page has: send to Transit (as a code block in the page’s language), a reminder on the selection, paste from the session clipboard, a date & time stamp written as a comment, copy the page, Export.
  • The status strip – line and column, lines and characters, indentation (click to change), CRLF / LF (click to switch), UTF-8, the language.
  • Export as Code – one entry; the Save dialog opens with the page’s language first in Save as type and every other format after it. UTF-8 (a PowerShell file with non-ASCII text gets a BOM, for Windows PowerShell 5.1).
  • Everything a page has – search finds the code, reminders anchor on it, the session clipboard pastes into it, exports leave an event on the dashboard, and AI tools read and write it over MCP (create_page with type code, get_page, update_page, append_to_page).

Dashboard

The dashboard – Quick View – is your workspace at a glance. Open it from the first button in the header; it also opens at every launch while Show at launch is on.

  • Structure and Content – how many topics, sub-topics, child topics, pages, spreadsheets, checklists and retained backups you have.
  • Storage & Backup – the User Data path, the backup path, the last successful backup and the next one.
  • Events – a searchable list of what happened: pages, topics, imports, backups, reminders and to-do changes.
  • To-do – total, overdue and done, with reminder cards you can tick off, edit or jump to.

Checklists, to-dos and reminders

Checklists

A checklist block has a title across the top and rows of tick box · item · notes, up to 100 rows. Create one from the toolbar, the / menu or right-click › Create checklist (with text selected: Create checklist from selection, one item per line). Enter adds a row, Tab / Shift+Tab walk the cells, the arrow keys move through and out of it, Ctrl+Enter or a click ticks a box, Backspace in an empty row removes it. Items nest three levels deep (Tab at the start of an item, or Ctrl+] / Ctrl+[); ticking an item ticks its sub-items. Right-click inside a checklist for a short menu: its reminders first, then add, tick, indent or remove an item, copy or delete the checklist. A checklist copies into Word, Outlook, OneNote and Teams as a table with its boxes.

Reminders

A reminder belongs to the text it was set on, and it takes two clicks to set: right-click, Create reminder…. Select text in a page first for a reminder on that text; in a checklist, Create reminder… points at the whole checklist (named after its title) and Create reminder on this item… at one item. Once a reminder is set, the same right-click offers Manage reminder… – one click back to its schedule. The text is marked with a dotted underline in its state colour; if the text moves to another page the reminder follows it, and if it is deleted the reminder is flagged “Content not found” rather than removed silently.

After a periodIn 1–365 days, 1–48 hours or 1–120 minutes
Exact timeA date and time; fires once
RecurringA start date, then every N days, hours or minutes

When a reminder is due, a pop-up rolls down over EasyNotes (or appears on its own when EasyNotes is hidden). Snooze it for minutes, hours or days – snoozing moves the pop-up, never the deadline – mark it complete, or switch the pop-up off for that reminder.

The to-do list in the header shows every reminder with a count of open items: a summary of open, overdue and done, a search box, and on each row a tick box, Edit the schedule, Go to content and Delete. Overdue items and items whose text has gone come first. The dashboard carries the same list.

Multi-select and Transit

Multi-select

Switch it on from the toolbar, the right-click menu or Ctrl+Shift+M, then pick up to 15 separate pieces of a page, in the order you want them: drag over words inside any paragraph, table cell, checklist item, callout or code block; click a picture, a shape, a rule, a contents block, an icon or a page link; click a block’s grip for the whole block; Ctrl+click words for the whole line. Each pick gets a number. Click a picked piece again to drop it, click its number badge, or use Undo pick; Clear drops them all and Done (Esc) leaves the mode. Every pick keeps what it held when picked, so later edits never change what you copy.

The bar then offers Copy (Ctrl+C or Enter – stacked in pick order, with formatting for Word, Outlook, OneNote and Teams and as plain text everywhere else), Transit, Checklist (one item per picked line), Paint (the loaded format painter onto every pick) and Replace every pick with some words, in one undo.

Transit

Transit is a parking pad for content on its way somewhere – to or from another app, or between pages. It opens from the header and closes with Esc, and it is a page itself: pictures, shapes, checklists, links and undo all work there. Send a selection with right-click › Send to Transit, send the multi-select picks with the bar’s Transit button, or send a session-clipboard item with its Transit button. A part of a list or table arrives as a proper list or table; pictures are copied into Transit’s own media, so they stay put if the page changes.

Transit is stored with your notes, so it is kept between sessions, included in backups and synced along with your notes folder. Clear empties it.

Session Clipboard

Switch on the session clipboard and EasyNotes keeps a list of what you copy in any app while it runs – text, links, files and folders, pictures – so an earlier copy can be pasted back into a page. Up to 300 items.

  • Only while EasyNotes is running – the list starts empty every time.
  • Kept on this device only, in a temporary folder in your Windows profile – never in your notes folder, never synced, never in backups, and deleted when EasyNotes exits (after a crash, at the next start).
  • Copies that password managers and other apps mark as private are skipped.
  • Switching it off stops recording at once; Clear all removes what is there.

Open the list from the header: click an item to paste it at the caret, or use its buttons to copy it back to the Windows clipboard, paste it into the open page (or into Transit while it is open), park it in Transit or remove it. In a page, right-click › Paste from clipboard… lets you pick several items and paste them in the order you picked them.

Base64. The Base64 switch (an icon in the clipboard list, in the paste picker and on the right-click menu’s Paste from clipboard row) pastes the Base64 value of what was copied instead of the item itself: text and links as their UTF-8 text, a picture as its PNG bytes, a copied file as its bytes (up to 10 MB per file). It is off every time EasyNotes starts.

World clock

Stay on top

The pin keeps EasyNotes above other windows while you copy and paste between apps. Ctrl+T toggles it, and so does the tray menu.

Dark theme

The moon switches between the light workspace and a soft blue-grey dark theme. Settings › Appearance can also follow Windows and use your Windows accent colour. Printing and PDF export always use the light colours.

Dim

Dim lays a warm tint over the light theme for long sessions or evening work. It applies to the light theme only – the dark theme is already easy on the eyes.
